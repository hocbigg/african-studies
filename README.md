## Introduction

African Studies is an interdisciplinary field that examines the histories, societies, cultures, and political economies of the African continent and its global diaspora. The discipline centers African agency, epistemologies, and institutional innovations, and it avoids framing the continent mainly through external intervention, crisis, or colonial categories. Studying it covers the continent's deep evolutionary prehistory, early state formation and trans-regional commercial networks, the disruptions of the slave trade and European colonialism, and the social transformations shaping modern African societies.

This curriculum is for independent learners approaching the discipline from the beginning. It assumes no prior background in African history, linguistics, anthropology, or international development. The core readings and lecture series introduce foundational terminology directly, from lineage systems and oral source criticism to neopatrimonialism and epistemic decolonization.

### Scope

This page covers the foundational core: the key concepts, historical milestones, and analytical debates that virtually every learner in African Studies needs to understand. It leaves out specialized elective areas, such as detailed language acquisition (e.g., Swahili, Yoruba, or Amharic grammar), regional subfield studies (such as North African security politics or Southern African mining legal histories), and advanced research methodologies.

After finishing the foundational subjects here, consult the [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md) guide for regional specializations and deeper theoretical debates, or use the [Readings](extras/readings.md) and [Courses](extras/courses.md) pages for primary source archives, practical case studies, and extended creative literature.

### How to Navigate the Curriculum

The curriculum starts with research frameworks, moves through a chronological history, and ends with thematic topics in contemporary African life:

- Epistemological and spatial foundations: Begin with *Scope & Epistemologies of African Studies*, *African Physical & Human Geography*, and *African Historiography & Methodologies*. These subjects cover how knowledge about Africa is produced, how regional ecologies shaped human settlement, and how scholars corroborate oral traditions, archaeology, and historical linguistics.
- The chronological historical spine: Work through the historical subjects in order, from *Human Origins & African Prehistory* and language migrations through early states, the slave trade eras, European partition, and anti-colonial resistance. Each era shaped the next, so following the sequence explains the origins of contemporary borders, demographic patterns, and institutional legacies.
- Contemporary thematic inquiries: After the historical spine through decolonization, the remaining subjects can be studied in any order: post-colonial governance, political economy, Pan-African diplomacy, religions and philosophies, literature, African feminisms, and urbanization. Each covers how historical structures interact with modern adaptations and policy challenges across the continent.

## Curriculum

### Scope & Epistemologies of African Studies

This subject introduces the multidisciplinary scope of African Studies and examines how the continent has been conceptualized, historicized, and categorized from external and indigenous perspectives.

[The African Experience: An Introduction (Vincent B. Khapoya / Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780205851713) - Start here for an accessible survey of the continent's geography, historical milestones, social institutions, and modern governance before moving to denser theoretical critiques.

[The Invention of Africa: Gnosis, Philosophy, and the Order of Knowledge (V. Y. Mudimbe / Indiana University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780253204684) - A challenging theoretical counterpart to Khapoya. It examines how colonial epistemologies and Western scholarship invented "Africa" as an object of study.

[Africa and the Politics of Knowledge (MIT OpenCourseWare / Prof. M. Amah Edoh)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21g-025-africa-and-the-politics-of-knowledge-spring-2019/) - A university reading syllabus that complements Mudimbe by showing how contemporary scholars analyze and decolonize knowledge production about the continent.

### African Physical & Human Geography

This subject covers Africa's physical topography, climatic zones, and environmental patterns, and how they interact with human settlement and spatial political economy, past and present.

[Africa South of the Sahara: A Geographical Interpretation (Robert Stock / Guilford Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781606239926) - The main in-depth textbook for the continent's physical landforms, ecological belts, disease environments, and spatial development.

[World Regional Geography: Sub-Saharan Africa (Caitlin Finlayson / Open Textbook Library)](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/world-regional-geography) - A free, open-access alternative to Stock. A single chapter gives a concise overview of the continent's climate zones, spatial organization, and demographic trends.

### African Historiography & Methodologies

This subject covers the methods and source criticism used to reconstruct African history, including oral traditions, archaeology, historical linguistics, and documentary records.

[Oral Tradition as History (Jan Vansina / University of Wisconsin Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780299102142) - The foundational methodology text for the discipline. It sets out rules for evaluating, dating, and corroborating oral testimony as historical evidence.

[General History of Africa, Vol. 1: Methodology and African Prehistory (UNESCO / Ed. Joseph Ki-Zerbo)](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000042225) - A free, open-access volume that complements Vansina by showing how African scholars combine archaeological, linguistic, and oral methods at a continental scale.

[The History of Africa with Zeinab Badawi (BBC News Africa / YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLajyiGz4JeyPq2lpEt2skZRhQsAspIQCp) - A video companion to the methodology readings. It takes learners to African historical sites where local historians and archaeologists show how sources are recovered.

### Human Origins & African Prehistory

This subject traces early hominin evolution, Stone Age technology, rock art traditions, and the emergence of settled food-producing communities across Africa.

[African Archaeology (David W. Phillipson / Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521540025) - The core narrative textbook for this topic, covering hominin emergence, Pleistocene tool industries, rock art complexes, and the transition to food production.

[Archaeology and Heritage of Africa (Coursera / Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona & DAI)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/archaeology-heritage-africa) - A free audit-mode video course to take alongside Phillipson, with lectures on hominin fossil sites, material culture excavations, and heritage conservation.

### African Languages, Peoples & Historical Migrations

This subject surveys Africa's linguistic landscape, classification systems, and the major demographic movements that shaped continental societies over millennia, most notably the Bantu expansion.

[African Languages: An Introduction (Eds. Bernd Heine & Derek Nurse / Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521666299) - Begin with the opening survey chapters of this linguistics text for the geographical distribution, classification criteria, and structural features of Africa's four major language phyla.

[The Civilizations of Africa: A History to 1800 (Christopher Ehret / University of Virginia Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780813928807) - Builds on the classifications in Heine and Nurse and shows how comparative linguistics and archaeology track ancient population movements, including the Bantu expansion.

### Early Civilizations, States & Trans-Regional Trade

This subject covers the political structures, economic systems, and cultural achievements of classical and medieval African civilizations, including Nubia, Aksum, the Swahili city-states, and the Sahelian empires.

[History of Africa (Kevin Shillington / Bloomsbury Academic)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781137504036) - The chronological backbone for this subject. Focus on chapters 4 through 12 for the governance, economies, and trade networks of early African kingdoms.

[Sundiata: An Epic of Old Mali (D. T. Niane / Pearson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781405849425) - A short Mandinka epic to read alongside Shillington. It is a primary source for how medieval Malian statecraft and historical memory were preserved through the oral griot tradition.

[Africa's Great Civilizations (PBS / Henry Louis Gates, Jr.)](https://www.pbs.org/show/africas-great-civilizations/) - A six-part documentary series that pairs with Shillington and shows the architecture, material culture, and trans-regional trade connections of classical and medieval polities.

### Slavery, Slave Trades & African Diasporas

This subject covers indigenous African forms of servitude, the demographic and institutional effects of the trans-Saharan and trans-Atlantic slave trades, and the dispersal of African peoples across the globe.

[Transformations in Slavery: A History of Slavery in Africa (Paul E. Lovejoy / Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521176187) - Start with this authoritative study of how internal African systems of servitude were changed, militarized, and expanded by external commercial trade.

[How Europe Underdeveloped Africa (Walter Rodney / Verso Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781788731188) - A political-economy critique to read alongside Lovejoy. It argues that external human extraction halted indigenous industrialization and locked the continent into an unequal global economy.

[Reversing Sail: A History of the African Diaspora (Michael A. Gomez / Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781108431873) - Widens the scope of Lovejoy and Rodney by tracing how dispersed African populations built resilient cultural, political, and religious communities across the Mediterranean, Islamic Asia, and the Americas.

### European Imperialism & Colonial Rule (1884-1960)

This subject covers the diplomatic partition of Africa, the imposition of colonial administrative and economic systems, and the ways African societies experienced, adapted to, and resisted foreign domination.

[African Perspectives on Colonialism (A. Adu Boahen / Johns Hopkins University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780801839313) - The best starting text, with four concise lectures on how African societies interpreted, negotiated, and militarily resisted European conquest during the Scramble for Africa.

[Citizen and Subject: Contemporary Africa and the Legacy of Late Colonialism (Mahmood Mamdani / Princeton University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780691180427) - Read after Boahen. This institutional critique explains how colonial indirect rule created a bifurcated state, with urban citizens governed by civil law and rural subjects ruled through customary power.

[Modern African History (MIT OpenCourseWare / Prof. Kenda Mutongi)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21h-165-modern-african-history-spring-2019/) - A reading syllabus and study guide to use as a roadmap alongside Boahen and Mamdani for colonial labor systems, administrative decrees, and social transformations.

### African Resistance, Nationalisms & Decolonization

This subject traces the intellectual, social, and political currents behind anti-colonial resistance, mass nationalist mobilization, armed liberation struggles, and the formal transfer of sovereignty.

[The Wretched of the Earth (Frantz Fanon / Grove Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780802158635) - The key theoretical manifesto of anti-colonial liberation. Prioritize "On Violence" and "The Pitfalls of National Consciousness" for Fanon's psychological analysis of colonial subjugation and his critique of national bourgeoisies.

[Africa since 1940: The Past of the Present (Frederick Cooper / Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781108727891) - The historical complement to Fanon. It charts how post-WWII urban labor strikes, peasant mobilizations, and constitutional negotiations forced colonial powers to give up formal control.

### Post-Colonial Politics & Governance

This subject explores the institutional trajectories of independent African states, including authoritarian rule, military intervention, clientelism, neopatrimonialism, and later waves of democratic reform.

[An Introduction to African Politics (Alex Thomson / Routledge)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780367468927) - The main foundational textbook. Through accessible comparative case studies, it introduces political science concepts such as neopatrimonialism, the military in politics, ethnic arithmetic, and democratic transitions.

[The State in Africa: The Politics of the Belly (Jean-François Bayart / Polity Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780745644370) - An advanced theoretical complement to Thomson on how informal patronage networks, elite assimilation, and external dependencies shape state authority beneath formal constitutional frameworks.

[The State of Africa: A History of the Continent Since Independence (Martin Meredith / Simon & Schuster)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781471196416) - A narrative companion to Thomson's theoretical framework, covering the major leaders, political upheavals, and civil conflicts in individual African states since independence.

[Democracy and Development: Perspectives from Africa (MITx / edX / Prof. Evan Lieberman)](https://www.edx.org/course/democracy-development-perspectives-mitx-17-571x) - A free audit-mode video course to watch alongside the readings, with lectures and discussions with African scholars and practitioners on electoral politics, public goods, and governance.

### Political Economy of African Development

This subject examines the structural challenges facing African economies, including commodity dependence, debt crises, structural adjustment programs, and debates over state-led versus market-driven development.

[Democracy and Development in Africa (Claude Ake / Brookings Institution Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780815702191) - Begin with this foundational critique. It argues that development stalled after independence because inherited authoritarian governance made people-centered development politically impossible.

[Poor Numbers: How We Are Misled by African Development Statistics and What to Do about It (Morten Jerven / Cornell University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780801478604) - A concise methodological critique to read after Ake. It exposes the data gaps, statistical distortions, and capacity shortages behind commonly cited African GDP and poverty figures.

[Our Continent, Our Future: African Perspectives on Structural Adjustment (Thandika Mkandawire & Charles C. Soludo / IDRC)](https://idrc-crdi.ca/en/book/our-continent-our-future-african-perspectives-structural-adjustment) - An open-access scholarly assessment to read alongside Ake and Jerven, evaluating how World Bank and IMF structural adjustment programs affected African state capacity, social spending, and economic autonomy.

### Pan-Africanism & African International Relations

This subject covers Pan-African political philosophy, regional integration, and the diplomatic strategies African states use to handle foreign intervention and assert agency in the global order.

[Africa Must Unite (Kwame Nkrumah / Panaf Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780901787033) - The foundational primary manifesto of continental Pan-Africanism. It makes the political case for continental economic and political unification to overcome neocolonial vulnerability and balkanization.

[Africa and the International System: The Politics of State Survival (Christopher Clapham / Cambridge University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521576680) - The main theoretical framework to read after Nkrumah. It analyzes how African governments with limited domestic reach used international sovereignty, diplomatic alliances, and external patronage to survive.

### African Religions & Philosophies

This subject covers indigenous cosmologies, the spread and adaptation of Islam and Christianity across the continent, communitarian ethics, and the major schools of modern African philosophy.

[African Religions: A Very Short Introduction (Jacob K. Olupona / Oxford University Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199790586) - Start here for a balanced, accessible survey of indigenous religious traditions, the historical enculturation of Islam and Christianity, and their social and political roles across Africa today.

[African Religions and Philosophy (John S. Mbiti / Heinemann)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780435895914) - The classic academic study to read after Olupona, with an in-depth look at traditional religious cosmologies, ancestral veneration, and ontological concepts of time.

[An Introduction to African Philosophy (Samuel Oluoch Imbo / Rowman & Littlefield)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780847688418) - Bridges religious cosmologies and formal philosophical inquiry, introducing ethnophilosophy, universalist versus particularist debates, and feminist philosophy.

[African Ethics (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy / Kwame Gyekye)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/african-ethics/) - A free, peer-reviewed overview that complements Imbo and Mbiti with a focused analysis of Ubuntu, moral personhood, and communitarian ethical frameworks.

### African Literature, Orature & Expressive Arts

This subject examines African literary traditions, oral poetics, language politics in creative writing, and popular expressive cultures from the colonial rupture to the present.

[The Danger of a Single Story (Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie / TED)](https://www.ted.com/talks/chimamanda_ngozi_adichie_the_danger_of_a_single_story) - A short recorded lecture to watch before the readings, on why multiple narratives are needed to dismantle reductive stereotypes about Africa.

[Things Fall Apart (Chinua Achebe / Anchor Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780385474542) - The foundational novel to read first. It depicts pre-colonial Igbo community life, complex social morality, and the cultural fracture caused by British colonial intervention.

[Decolonising the Mind: The Politics of Language in African Literature (Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o / James Currey)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780852555019) - The critical counterpart to Achebe's fiction, arguing that cultural and intellectual decolonization requires writing in indigenous African languages rather than colonial ones.

[Global Africa: Creative Cultures (MIT OpenCourseWare / Prof. M. Amah Edoh)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21g-026-global-africa-creative-cultures-spring-2018/) - An open university syllabus for extending literary study into contemporary cinema, popular music, visual arts, and fashion.

### Kinship, Gender & African Feminisms

This subject explores pre-colonial family structures, matriarchy, and the social construction of gender in Africa, and contrasts indigenous organizational principles with Western feminist frameworks.

[The Invention of Women: Making an African Sense of Western Gender Discourses (Oyèrónkẹ́ Oyěwùmí / University of Minnesota Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780816624416) - The core theoretical monograph to study first. Oyěwùmí argues that seniority, not anatomical sex, organized pre-colonial Yoruba society, and that "woman" as a subordinate category was introduced under colonialism.

[African Gender Studies: A Reader (Ed. Oyèrónkẹ́ Oyěwùmí / Palgrave Macmillan)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781403962836) - An anthology to read alongside Oyěwùmí's monograph, collecting historical and anthropological essays on African motherhood, kinship, and female political authority.

[Decolonization and Afro-Feminism (Sylvia Tamale / Daraja Press)](https://darajapress.com/publication/decolonziation_and_afro-feminism) - A free open-access contemporary study that builds on Oyěwùmí and applies an Afro-feminist lens to legal institutions, human rights debates, bodily autonomy, and sexual politics in modern Africa.

### Urbanization, Demographics & Social Change

This subject analyzes the rapid growth of African cities, informal economic systems, youth culture, and the social adaptations that arise from urban precarity.

[African cities : An Introduction to Urban Planning (Coursera / École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/african-cities1) - A free audit-mode video course offering a structured orientation to African urban growth patterns, spatial dynamics, and urban infrastructure.

[New Urban Worlds: Inhabiting Dissonant Times (AbdouMaliq Simone & Edgar Pieterse / Polity Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780745691565) - The main sociological text for this topic. It examines how residents of rapidly expanding African cities handle economic informality and infrastructural deficits through informal social networks.

[The Time of Youth: Work, Social Change, and Politics in Africa (Alcinda Honwana / Kumarian Press)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781565494725) - Read alongside Simone and Pieterse for the youth bulge and the experience of "waithood" that drives youth migration and political mobilization.
