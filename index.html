Hocbigg - African Studies

Last updated on: 2026-09-16

Introduction

African Studies is an interdisciplinary field that examines the histories, societies, cultures, and political economies of the African continent and its global diaspora. Rather than viewing the continent through reductive narratives of external intervention, crisis, or colonial categorization, the discipline centers African agency, epistemologies, and institutional innovations. Studying African Studies provides a rigorous grounding in the continent's deep evolutionary prehistory, early state formations and trans-regional commercial networks, the ruptures of the slave trade and European colonialism, and the ongoing social transformations shaping modern African societies.

This curriculum is intended for independent learners approaching the discipline from the beginning. No prior background in African history, linguistics, anthropology, or international development is assumed. Foundational terminology — ranging from lineage systems and oral source criticism to neopatrimonialism and epistemic decolonization — is introduced directly through the core readings and lecture series.

Scope

This page focuses on the foundational core: the key concepts, historical milestones, and analytical debates that virtually every learner in African Studies must understand. It purposefully excludes specialized elective areas, such as detailed language acquisition (e.g., Swahili, Yoruba, or Amharic grammar), specialized regional subfield studies (such as North African security politics or Southern African mining legal histories), and advanced academic research methodologies.

Once you have mastered the foundational subjects presented here, you can consult the Advanced Topics guide to explore regional specializations and deeper theoretical debates, or draw upon the Readings, and Courses pages to engage with primary source archives, practical case studies, and extended creative literature.

How to Navigate the Curriculum

The curriculum is structured to guide you from foundational research frameworks through a chronological historical sequence, followed by thematic inquiries into contemporary African life:

  • Epistemological and Spatial Foundations: Begin with Scope & Epistemologies of African Studies, African Physical & Human Geography, and African Historiography & Methodologies. These subjects provide the conceptual and environmental tools necessary for serious study, establishing how knowledge about Africa is produced, how regional ecologies shaped human settlement, and how scholars rigorously corroborate oral traditions, archaeology, and historical linguistics.
  • The Chronological Historical Spine: Work through the historical subjects in sequential order, from Human Origins & African Prehistory and language migrations through early states, the slave trade eras, European partition, and anti-colonial resistance. Because each era structurally transformed the next, following this chronological sequence ensures you understand the historical precedents behind contemporary borders, demographic patterns, and institutional legacies.
  • Contemporary Thematic Inquiries: After completing the historical spine through decolonization, the remaining subjects—spanning post-colonial governance, political economy, Pan-African diplomacy, religions and philosophies, literature, African feminisms, and urbanization—can be studied in any order that suits your interests. Each topic addresses how historical structures interact with modern adaptations and policy challenges across the continent.

Curriculum

Scope & Epistemologies of African Studies

This subject introduces the multidisciplinary scope of African Studies, examining how the African continent has been conceptualized, historicized, and categorized from both external and indigenous perspectives.

The African Experience: An Introduction (Vincent B. Khapoya / Routledge) - Start here for an accessible baseline survey of the continent's geography, historical milestones, social institutions, and modern governance before engaging with denser theoretical critiques.

The Invention of Africa: Gnosis, Philosophy, and the Order of Knowledge (V. Y. Mudimbe / Indiana University Press) - A challenging theoretical counterpart to Khapoya that examines how colonial epistemologies and Western scholarship historically invented "Africa" as an intellectual object of study.

Africa and the Politics of Knowledge (MIT OpenCourseWare / Prof. M. Amah Edoh) - A curated university reading syllabus that complements Mudimbe by demonstrating how contemporary scholars analyze and decolonize knowledge production about the continent.

African Physical & Human Geography

This subject examines Africa's physical topography, climatic zones, and environmental patterns alongside their historical and contemporary interactions with human settlement and spatial political economy.

Africa South of the Sahara: A Geographical Interpretation (Robert Stock / Guilford Press) - The primary, in-depth textbook choice for studying the continent's physical landforms, ecological belts, disease environments, and spatial development.

World Regional Geography: Sub-Saharan Africa (Caitlin Finlayson / Open Textbook Library) - A free, open-access alternative to Stock that provides a concise introductory overview of the continent's climate zones, spatial organization, and demographic trends in a single chapter.

African Historiography & Methodologies

This subject explores the methodologies and source criticism necessary for reconstructing African history, focusing on the integration of oral traditions, archaeology, historical linguistics, and documentary records.

Oral Tradition as History (Jan Vansina / University of Wisconsin Press) - The foundational methodology text for the discipline, establishing critical rules for evaluating, dating, and corroborating oral testimonies as historical evidence.

General History of Africa, Vol. 1: Methodology and African Prehistory (UNESCO / Ed. Joseph Ki-Zerbo) - A free, open-access foundational volume that complements Vansina by demonstrating how African scholars synthesize archaeological, linguistic, and oral methodologies at a continental scale.

The History of Africa with Zeinab Badawi (BBC News Africa / YouTube) - A visual field companion to the methodology readings, taking learners to African historical sites where local historians and archaeologists demonstrate source recovery in practice.

Human Origins & African Prehistory

This subject traces early hominin evolution, Stone Age technological development, rock art traditions, and the emergence of settled food-producing communities across the African continent.

African Archaeology (David W. Phillipson / Cambridge University Press) - The core narrative textbook for this topic, detailing hominin emergence, Pleistocene tool industries, rock art complexes, and the transition to food production.

Archaeology and Heritage of Africa (Coursera / Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona & DAI) - A free audit-mode video course to take alongside Phillipson, offering visual lectures on hominin fossil sites, material culture excavations, and heritage conservation.

African Languages, Peoples & Historical Migrations

This subject surveys Africa's linguistic landscape, classification systems, and the major demographic movements—most notably the Bantu expansion—that shaped continental societies over millennia.

African Languages: An Introduction (Eds. Bernd Heine & Derek Nurse / Cambridge University Press) - Begin with the opening survey chapters of this linguistics text to grasp the geographical distribution, classification criteria, and structural features of Africa's four major language phyla.

The Civilizations of Africa: A History to 1800 (Christopher Ehret / University of Virginia Press) - Builds directly on the linguistic classifications introduced by Heine and Nurse, demonstrating how comparative linguistics and archaeology track ancient population movements and the continental Bantu expansion.

Early Civilizations, States & Trans-Regional Trade

This subject investigates the political structures, economic systems, and cultural achievements of classical and medieval African civilizations, including Nubia, Aksum, the Swahili city-states, and the Sahelian empires.

History of Africa (Kevin Shillington / Bloomsbury Academic) - The central chronological spine for this subject; focus on chapters 4 through 12 to study the governance, economies, and trade networks of early African kingdoms.

Sundiata: An Epic of Old Mali (D. T. Niane / Pearson) - A short Mandinka epic to read alongside Shillington, providing an indispensable primary-source window into how medieval Malian statecraft and historical memory were preserved through oral griot tradition.

Africa's Great Civilizations (PBS / Henry Louis Gates, Jr.) - A six-part visual documentary series that pairs with Shillington to illustrate the architecture, material culture, and trans-regional trade connections of classical and medieval polities.

Slavery, Slave Trades & African Diasporas

This subject analyzes indigenous African forms of servitude, the demographic and institutional impacts of the trans-Saharan and trans-Atlantic slave trades, and the dispersal of African peoples across the globe.

Transformations in Slavery: A History of Slavery in Africa (Paul E. Lovejoy / Cambridge University Press) - Start with this authoritative historical study to understand how internal African systems of servitude were altered, militarized, and expanded by external commercial trade.

How Europe Underdeveloped Africa (Walter Rodney / Verso Books) - A foundational political-economy critique to read alongside Lovejoy, arguing that external human extraction halted indigenous industrialization and structurally locked the continent into an unequal global economy.

Reversing Sail: A History of the African Diaspora (Michael A. Gomez / Cambridge University Press) - Expands the historical scope of Lovejoy and Rodney by tracing how dispersed African populations built resilient cultural, political, and religious communities across the Mediterranean, Islamic Asia, and the Americas.

European Imperialism & Colonial Rule (1884–1960)

This subject examines the diplomatic partition of Africa, the imposition of colonial administrative and economic systems, and the varied ways African societies experienced, adapted to, and resisted foreign domination.

African Perspectives on Colonialism (A. Adu Boahen / Johns Hopkins University Press) - The ideal starting text, delivering four concise lectures that articulate how African societies interpreted, negotiated, and militarily resisted European conquest during the Scramble for Africa.

Citizen and Subject: Contemporary Africa and the Legacy of Late Colonialism (Mahmood Mamdani / Princeton University Press) - Read after Boahen for a structural institutional critique explaining how colonial indirect rule created a bifurcated state separating urban citizens governed by civil law from rural subjects ruled through customary power.

Modern African History (MIT OpenCourseWare / Prof. Kenda Mutongi) - A structured reading syllabus and study guide to use as an organizational roadmap alongside Boahen and Mamdani for analyzing colonial labor systems, administrative decrees, and social transformations.

African Resistance, Nationalisms & Decolonization

This subject traces the intellectual, social, and political currents that fueled anti-colonial resistance, mass nationalist mobilizations, armed liberation struggles, and the formal transfer of political sovereignty.

The Wretched of the Earth (Frantz Fanon / Grove Press) - The essential theoretical manifesto of anti-colonial liberation; prioritize "On Violence" and "The Pitfalls of National Consciousness" to examine Fanon's psychological analysis of colonial subjugation and his critique of national bourgeoisies.

Africa since 1940: The Past of the Present (Frederick Cooper / Cambridge University Press) - The grounded historical complement to Fanon, methodically charting how post-WWII urban labor strikes, peasant mobilizations, and constitutional negotiations forced colonial powers to relinquish formal control.

Post-Colonial Politics & Governance

This subject explores the institutional trajectories of independent African states, focusing on authoritarian rule, military intervention, clientelism, neopatrimonialism, and subsequent waves of democratic reform.

An Introduction to African Politics (Alex Thomson / Routledge) - The primary foundational textbook, systematically introducing essential political science concepts such as neopatrimonialism, the military in politics, ethnic arithmetic, and democratic transitions through accessible comparative case studies.

The State in Africa: The Politics of the Belly (Jean-François Bayart / Polity Press) - An advanced theoretical complement to Thomson that explores how informal patronage networks, elite assimilation, and external dependencies shape state authority beneath formal constitutional frameworks.

The State of Africa: A History of the Continent Since Independence (Martin Meredith / Simon & Schuster) - An accessible, narrative-driven companion to Thomson's theoretical framework, chronicling the major leaders, political upheavals, and civil conflicts across individual African states since independence.

Democracy and Development: Perspectives from Africa (MITx / edX / Prof. Evan Lieberman) - A free audit-mode video course to watch alongside the readings, featuring lectures and discussions with African scholars and practitioners on electoral politics, public goods, and governance.

Political Economy of African Development

This subject examines the structural challenges facing African economies, including commodity dependence, debt crises, structural adjustment programs, and debates over state-led versus market-driven development.

Democracy and Development in Africa (Claude Ake / Brookings Institution Press) - Begin with this foundational critique, which argues that economic development stalled in post-independence Africa because inherited authoritarian governance structures made genuine, people-centered development politically impossible.

Poor Numbers: How We Are Misled by African Development Statistics and What to Do about It (Morten Jerven / Cornell University Press) - A concise methodological critique to read after Ake, exposing the severe data gaps, statistical distortions, and capacity shortages behind commonly cited African GDP and poverty indicators.

Our Continent, Our Future: African Perspectives on Structural Adjustment (Thandika Mkandawire & Charles C. Soludo / IDRC) - An open-access scholarly assessment to read alongside Ake and Jerven, evaluating how World Bank and IMF structural adjustment programs affected African state capacity, social spending, and economic autonomy.

Pan-Africanism & African International Relations

This subject investigates Pan-African political philosophy, regional integration, and the diplomatic strategies African states deploy to navigate foreign intervention and assert agency within the global order.

Africa Must Unite (Kwame Nkrumah / Panaf Books) - The foundational primary manifesto of continental Pan-Africanism, presenting the urgent political case for continental economic and political unification to overcome neocolonial vulnerability and balkanization.

Africa and the International System: The Politics of State Survival (Christopher Clapham / Cambridge University Press) - The primary theoretical framework to read after Nkrumah, analyzing how African governments with fragile domestic reach leveraged international sovereignty, diplomatic alliances, and external patronage for state survival.

African Religions & Philosophies

This subject investigates indigenous cosmologies, the continental spread and adaptation of Islam and Christianity, communitarian ethics, and the major schools of thought in modern African philosophy.

African Religions: A Very Short Introduction (Jacob K. Olupona / Oxford University Press) - Start here for a balanced, accessible survey of indigenous religious traditions, the historical enculturation of Islam and Christianity, and their contemporary social and political roles across Africa.

African Religions and Philosophy (John S. Mbiti / Heinemann) - The classic academic study to read after Olupona, providing an in-depth examination of traditional religious cosmologies, ancestral veneration, and ontological concepts of time.

An Introduction to African Philosophy (Samuel Oluoch Imbo / Rowman & Littlefield) - Bridges religious cosmologies with formal philosophical inquiry, systematically introducing ethnophilosophy, universalist versus particularist debates, and feminist philosophy.

African Ethics (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy / Kwame Gyekye) - A free, peer-reviewed overview that complements Imbo and Mbiti with a focused analysis of Ubuntu, moral personhood, and communitarian ethical frameworks.

African Literature, Orature & Expressive Arts

This subject examines African literary traditions, oral poetics, language politics in creative writing, and popular expressive cultures from the colonial rupture to the contemporary era.

The Danger of a Single Story (Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie / TED) - A short recorded lecture to watch before diving into the readings, highlighting why narrative multiplicity is necessary to dismantle reductive stereotypes about Africa.

Things Fall Apart (Chinua Achebe / Anchor Books) - The foundational literary novel to read first, depicting pre-colonial Igbo community life, complex social morality, and the tragic cultural fracture caused by British colonial intervention.

Decolonising the Mind: The Politics of Language in African Literature (Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o / James Currey) - The essential critical counterpart to Achebe's fiction, arguing that genuine cultural and intellectual decolonization requires writing in indigenous African languages rather than colonial ones.

Global Africa: Creative Cultures (MIT OpenCourseWare / Prof. M. Amah Edoh) - An open university syllabus to consult as an exploratory guide for extending literary study into contemporary cinema, popular music, visual arts, and fashion.

Kinship, Gender & African Feminisms

This subject explores pre-colonial family structures, matriarchy, and the social construction of gender in Africa, contrasting indigenous organizational principles with Western feminist frameworks.

The Invention of Women: Making an African Sense of Western Gender Discourses (Oyèrónkẹ́ Oyěwùmí / University of Minnesota Press) - The core theoretical monograph to study first; Oyěwùmí demonstrates that seniority rather than anatomical sex organized pre-colonial Yoruba society, and that "woman" as a subordinate category was introduced under colonialism.

African Gender Studies: A Reader (Ed. Oyèrónkẹ́ Oyěwùmí / Palgrave Macmillan) - A comprehensive anthology to read alongside Oyěwùmí's monograph, collecting diverse historical and anthropological essays on African motherhood, kinship, and female political authority.

Decolonization and Afro-Feminism (Sylvia Tamale / Daraja Press) - A free open-access contemporary study that builds directly on Oyěwùmí, applying an Afro-feminist lens to legal institutions, human rights debates, bodily autonomy, and sexual politics in modern Africa.

Urbanization, Demographics & Social Change

This subject analyzes the rapid growth of African cities, informal economic systems, youth culture, and the social adaptations forged in response to urban precarity.

African cities : An Introduction to Urban Planning (Coursera / École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne) - A free audit-mode video course providing a structured orientation to African urban growth patterns, spatial dynamics, and urban infrastructure.

New Urban Worlds: Inhabiting Dissonant Times (AbdouMaliq Simone & Edgar Pieterse / Polity Press) - The primary sociological text for this topic, examining how residents in rapidly expanding African cities navigate economic informality and infrastructural deficits through informal social networks.

The Time of Youth: Work, Social Change, and Politics in Africa (Alcinda Honwana / Kumarian Press) - Read alongside Simone and Pieterse to focus specifically on the demographic youth bulge and the socioeconomic experience of "waithood" that drives youth migration and political mobilization.
